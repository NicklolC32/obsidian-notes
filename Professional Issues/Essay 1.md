**"How might your work on this project contribute to unintentional harm?**

- data analysis Report of Housing Crisis in Edinburgh
#### Potential Harms:
1) available to the public and aimed towards students from Edinburgh - survey allows anonymity (protects personal privacy, ACM 1.6 and 1.7) HOWEVER risk of innacuracy because this may be a reason for people to lie or provide misinterpreted answers due to lack of motivation therefore not answering properly (*response bias 1* - extreme responding or acquiescence bias)
	- so misleading report
	- undermines and questions credibility and validity
	- conflicts with ACM: 1.3 Be honest and trustworthy

> [!NOTE] Terminology
> *Acquiescence bias* - is the tendancy for ppts to say "yes" to questions or to agree rather than disagree. Shows up in binary response format questions. These formats can **subtly encourage agreement, even when the ppt doesn't feel strongly or doesn't fully understand the question**.
>
*Extreme response bias* - is when ppts consistently choose the most extreme answer options on a scale such as "strongly agree" or "strongly disagree" **regardless of the question context or even if their actual opinion is more moderate**. This can happen when the question's wording leans to have a preferred answer, a desire to please the surveyor, or general disengagement with the survey.

**Counterpoint**
- limits to how we can mitigate risk
- anonymity is important regardlesss and can actually encourage honesty
- disclosure/non-disclosure of identity - option to remain anonymous but data can sometimes be retraceable especially if the survey is based on students' living conditions
	- **no guaranteed of full anonymity** therefore when using written answers, must properly hide details that could expose identity
